If home value decreases key risk
Still pays contracted %
Share of home value. Unlock still collects its full contracted percentage of ending home value — the dollar amount is lower only because the home is worth less, not because Unlock waives or reduces its rate. "If your home's ending value is lower, it will reduce Unlock's share" — meaning Unlock's dollar amount is lower, but Unlock's percentage share is unchanged. Homeowner is not protected from the rate itself.
unlock.com/faqs (primary source)
Pays original investment + % above discounted baseline
Share of appreciation model. Point uses a risk-adjusted starting value (~27% below appraised value). In a moderate depreciation, your home may still be above Point's discounted baseline — meaning you still owe Point their appreciation share on top of the original investment. Your home must fall significantly below the discounted baseline before Point takes a loss. Worst depreciation protection of the six due to the starting value discount.
point.com/hei/how-hei-works; point.com/blog/share-of-value-vs-share-of-appreciation
Still pays contracted % of ending value
Under Hometap's June 2026 pricing structure, the multiplier is fixed at 1.65× (years 0–5) and 1.80× (years 6–10) regardless of whether the home value goes up or down. The multiplier does not change based on direction of home value movement — only based on time. If your home depreciates, the dollar amount you owe is lower only because the home is worth less, not because Hometap reduces its rate. Confirmed on hometap.com's own pricing page: in a depreciation scenario, "Hometap Share: What the homeowner pays to settle, which is the lesser of the Hometap Percentage and Hometap Cap" — in a down market the Cap is always higher, so the homeowner pays the Percentage at the same multiplier. The 18.5% annual cap protects against runaway costs in appreciation scenarios only.
Jim Riccitelli, CEO Unlock Technologies (overruling third-party sources); hometap.com/blog/how-hometap-pricing-works (primary source confirmed)
Still pays contracted % of ending value
Share of home value. Splitero collects its contracted percentage of whatever the home is worth at settlement. If home depreciates, the dollar amount owed is lower — but Splitero's rate is unchanged. Exact depreciation scenarios not published. Splitero's "Safety Cap" only limits upside in appreciation — it does not reduce the rate in a down market.
Splitero/Blue Owl press release; equitysquirrel.com Point vs Splitero July 2026
Still pays contracted % of ending value

nada.co/pricing (primary source)

Share of home value. Regulatory environment industry-wide
⚖️ Home Equity Lending Integrity Act (S. 4803) — introduced June 17, 2026 by Senator Jeff Merkley
Proposes to amend the Truth in Lending Act (TILA) to classify home equity investments as residential mortgages, extending federal consumer protections to HEI/HEA products. Currently HEIs operate without a federal regulatory framework. The Coalition for Home Equity Partnership (CHEP) — of which Unlock, Point, and Hometap are members — has responded that shared equity products are not compatible with the definition of a traditional mortgage as they have no loan balance, interest rate, or monthly payments. State-level regulatory engagement is also increasing. This legislation, if passed, would significantly change how all six companies operate, originate, and disclose their products. Monitor closely.
Money.com August 2026; Senator Merkley press release June 17, 2026

Who Has the Best Deal for the Homeowner?

Weighted scorecard across six HEI/HEA companies — evaluated from the consumer's perspective | August 2026

📐 Scoring Methodology
Factor Weight Why it matters for consumers
Credit pull (hard vs. soft) 20% A hard pull can reduce a consumer's credit score at a vulnerable financial moment. Consumers exploring equity options are often also considering refinancing or other credit products — a hard pull has real downstream consequences.
Term length flexibility 20% A longer or more flexible term gives consumers more time to settle without being forced to sell or refinance at an inopportune moment. Co-term products that match the mortgage maturity are particularly consumer-friendly.
Total cost of agreement 20% Includes exchange rate/multiplier, annual cost cap, pricing model (share of value vs. appreciation), and home value discount at origination. This is the most important long-term cost factor — small differences in rate compound significantly over 10+ years.
Origination & third-party fees 15% Upfront costs reduce the net amount the consumer receives. On a $100K investment, a 1% fee difference equals $1,000 less in hand at closing. Also considers whether fees are transparent and disclosed upfront.
Width of the credit box 25% Weighted highest because access matters most — a product that excludes more consumers is less valuable to the market regardless of its terms. Includes minimum FICO, max CLTV, lien position flexibility, property types, rental/investment eligibility, bankruptcy/foreclosure seasoning, and unsecured debt requirements.
