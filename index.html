Who Has the Best Deal for the Homeowner?

Weighted scorecard across six HEI/HEA companies — evaluated from the consumer's perspective | August 2026

📐 Scoring Methodology
Factor Weight Why it matters for consumers
Credit pull (hard vs. soft) 20% A hard pull can reduce a consumer's credit score at a vulnerable financial moment. Consumers exploring equity options are often also considering refinancing or other credit products — a hard pull has real downstream consequences.
Term length flexibility 20% A longer or more flexible term gives consumers more time to settle without being forced to sell or refinance at an inopportune moment. Co-term products that match the mortgage maturity are particularly consumer-friendly.
Total cost of agreement 20% Includes exchange rate/multiplier, annual cost cap, pricing model (share of value vs. appreciation), and home value discount at origination. This is the most important long-term cost factor — small differences in rate compound significantly over 10+ years.
Origination & third-party fees 15% Upfront costs reduce the net amount the consumer receives. On a $100K investment, a 1% fee difference equals $1,000 less in hand at closing. Also considers whether fees are transparent and disclosed upfront.
Width of the credit box 25% Weighted highest because access matters most — a product that excludes more consumers is less valuable to the market regardless of its terms. Includes minimum FICO, max CLTV, lien position flexibility, property types, rental/investment eligibility, bankruptcy/foreclosure seasoning, and unsecured debt requirements.

🏆 Summary Rankings — Best Deal for the Homeowner
🥇
Unlock — 4.45 / 5.00
BEST OVERALL DEAL FOR CONSUMERS
The only company with an unconditional soft-pull guarantee. Widest credit box through the FLEX and FIRST products — accepts 500 FICO, delinquent mortgages, rental properties, and 1st lien position. 10–30 year co-term prevents forced exits. The combination of access (who can get it) and flexibility (how long you have) is unmatched in the category. Scores lower only on total cost vs. Nada due to slightly higher exchange rate starting point.
🥈
Nada — 3.65 / 5.00
BEST COST STRUCTURE
Best exchange rate (1.85×) and simple annual cap (19.99%) of any company — transparently published. Near-soft-pull (conditional hard only). But 14-state footprint severely limits consumer reach, dragging the overall score. If Nada expands nationally it becomes a much stronger consumer proposition.
🥉
Hometap — 2.65 / 5.00
BEST BRAND TRUST / CONSUMER EXPERIENCE
4.8★ from 6,000+ Trustpilot reviews is the strongest consumer trust signal in the category. Detailed fee disclosure post-application. Strong content and education resources. But the 10-year firm term, highest effective annual cap (~20.2%), 575 FICO minimum, and hard pull requirement limit the consumer value proposition relative to Unlock and Nada.
4th
Splitero — 2.60 / 5.00
BEST TERM FLEXIBILITY (tied)
Co-term flexibility matching mortgage maturity is genuine consumer value. But the $50K minimum investment excludes a large share of the market, pricing opacity is a real consumer risk, highest total third-party fee burden, and no rental/investment properties limit its reach. Fiat Growth's distribution advantages are supplier-side, not consumer-side.
5th
Point — 2.90 / 5.00
BEST FEES / LARGEST FOOTPRINT
Largest state footprint (29 + DC) and lowest advertised fee (3.9%) are genuine strengths. 30-year term is consumer-friendly for long-horizon homeowners. But the 27% home value discount on starting value is the worst total cost structure in the category — it significantly offsets the fee advantage on most investments. Share of appreciation model benefits Point more than the consumer in typical market conditions.
6th
JG Wentworth — 2.35 / 5.00
WEAKEST OVERALL CONSUMER PROPOSITION
Highest minimum FICO (600), lowest max investment ($400K), highest exchange rate (2.0×), most aggressive co-ownership enforcement language, and limited HEA-specific licensing (4 states). The HEA product appears early-stage and the entire company's brand equity sits in structured settlements and debt relief — not home equity. No meaningful differentiator for consumers vs. the other five companies.
